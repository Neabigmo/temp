\section{A uniform Laguerre estimate}\label{app:laguerre}

For $\alpha=1/2$, use the normalization
\[
\mathcal L_m^{(\alpha)}(r)
 =a_{m,\alpha}L_m^{(\alpha)}(r)e^{-r/2}r^{\alpha/2},
\qquad
a_{m,\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{m!}{\Gamma(m+\alpha+1)}},
\qquad
\nu=4m+2\alpha+2.
\]
Proposition~3.2 of \cite{ImekrazRobertThomann2016} gives
\[
|\mathcal L_m^{(1/2)}(r)|\le C(r\nu)^{1/4}\quad (0\le r\le\nu^{-1}),
\]
\[
|\mathcal L_m^{(1/2)}(r)|\le C(r\nu)^{-1/4}\quad (\nu^{-1}\le r\le\nu/2),
\]
\[
|\mathcal L_m^{(1/2)}(r)|
 \le C\nu^{-1/4}\bigl(\nu^{1/3}+|\nu-r|\bigr)^{-1/4}
 \quad (\nu/2\le r\le3\nu/2),
\]
and an exponential estimate for $r\ge3\nu/2$. Since
\[
\sqrt r\,e^{-r/2}|L_m^{(1/2)}(r)|
 =\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(m+3/2)}{m!}}\,r^{1/4}|\mathcal L_m^{(1/2)}(r)|,
\]
the two initial regions are uniformly bounded after multiplication by $e^{-r/6}$. In the turning region the same multiplication contributes $e^{-r/6}\le e^{-\nu/12}$, which dominates the polynomial factor from the displayed estimate, and the outer estimate is exponentially decaying. Consequently,
\[
\sup_{m\ge1,\,r\ge0}\sqrt r\,e^{-2r/3}|L_m^{(1/2)}(r)|<\infty.
\]
