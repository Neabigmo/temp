We prove a Gaussian rigidity principle based on the symmetrized moment generating function $M_X(s)M_X(-s)$. First, let $X=(X_i)$ have independent centered coordinates and let $A\succeq0$. If $X^{\mathsf T}AX$ has a chi-square law and each active coordinate satisfies the local dominance $M_i(s)M_i(-s)\ge e^{\sigma_i^2s^2}$, then every active coordinate is Gaussian. Second, under a fixed exponential-moment bound, the local estimate
\[
\sup_{|s|\le\rho}|\log M_X(s)+\log M_X(-s)-s^2|\le\Delta
\]
implies
\[
d_{\mathrm K}(X,\mathcal N(0,1))
\le C\sqrt{\frac{\log\log(1/\Delta)}{\log(1/\Delta)}}.
\]
The order is optimal in general, as shown by a Laguerre construction. The proof recovers the factor from reflected MGF information through a growing zero-free region. We also derive exact and quantitative consequences for the sample variance and show that total-variation stability fails.
