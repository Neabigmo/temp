\section{From radial discrepancy to a pointwise reflected defect}\label{sec:radial}

For the sample-variance application define
\[
D(s):=\frac12\log\frac{M_X(s)M_X(-s)}{e^{s^2}}\ge0.
\]
The radial-transform comparison yields an integrated spherical bound of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integrated-D}
n\,\E D\!\left(\tau\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n}}V_1\right)
\le \log(1+\varepsilon),
\end{equation}
where $V_1$ is the first coordinate of a uniform point on $S^{n-2}$. Its density is
\[
f_{V_1}(v)=
\frac{\Gamma((n-1)/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma((n-2)/2)}
(1-v^2)^{(n-4)/2},\qquad -1<v<1.
\]
For $n=3$ this is the arcsine density.

The exponential-integrability assumption gives the uniform derivative bound
\[
|D'(s)|\le \sqrt K+|s|.
\]
On every strict interior interval $|s|\le\rho'<\tau\sqrt{(n-1)/n}$, the density of the spherical coordinate is bounded below. A nonnegative Lipschitz tent argument applied to \eqref{eq:integrated-D} therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pointwise-D}
\sup_{|s|\le\rho'}D(s)
\le C\sqrt{\varepsilon}
\end{equation}
for small $\varepsilon$, with $C$ depending only on the fixed parameters.

\begin{remark}
In the tent inversion one must solve in the direction $\eta\ge2m_h I$ (equivalently $y=\eta/(2m_h)$ in the project notation); reversing this inequality gives the wrong pointwise bound.
\end{remark}
