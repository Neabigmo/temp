\section{Discussion and open directions}\label{sec:discussion}

The sharp reflection rate is slower than what is available in equal-factor Cram\'er stability because reflection erases the characteristic-function phase. The present proof recovers the phase indirectly by manufacturing a growing zero-free disk after a square-root-scale truncation. This mechanism may be useful in other factor-recovery problems in which only a modulus or autocorrelation is initially observed.

The unrestricted fixed-$n$ sample-variance characterization remains outside the scope of the paper: our exact and quantitative results use local symmetrized-MGF dominance. Removing that assumption would require a new mechanism for controlling the odd/phase degrees of freedom.

Several exploratory descriptions---Jacobi square budgets, Toeplitz--Schur chains, and Rogers--Szeg\H{o} certificates---help explain the factorial $m\log m$ scale but are not needed in the streamlined proof. They may be developed separately if they lead to an independent structural theorem.
