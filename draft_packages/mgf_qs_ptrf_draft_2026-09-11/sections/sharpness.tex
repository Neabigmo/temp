\section{Optimality and obstruction results}\label{sec:sharpness}

\subsection{A Laguerre lower-bound family}

We give the lower-bound construction in full because it identifies the scale in Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-upper}. The only external input is the four-region Laguerre estimate in Proposition~3.2 of Imekraz, Robert and Thomann \cite{ImekrazRobertThomann2016}. In their normalization,
\[
\mathcal L_m^{(\alpha)}(r)
 =\sqrt{\frac{m!}{\Gamma(m+\alpha+1)}}e^{-r/2}r^{\alpha/2}L_m^{(\alpha)}(r),
\qquad \nu=4m+2\alpha+2.
\]
For $\alpha=1/2$, the four bounds on $[0,1/\nu]$, $[1/\nu,\nu/2]$, $[\nu/2,3\nu/2]$, and $[3\nu/2,\infty)$ imply, after multiplication by the gamma ratio, that
\[
Q_m(r):=\sqrt r\,e^{-r/2}|L_m^{(1/2)}(r)|
\le C\quad (r\le\nu/2),
\qquad
Q_m(r)\le C\nu^{1/6}\quad (r\ge\nu/2).
\]
The second bound is only polynomially growing in the turning region, but the extra exponential factor below removes it. Since $r=3x^2/2$,
\[
\left|x e^{-x^2}L_m^{(1/2)}(3x^2/2)\right|
 =\sqrt{\frac23}\,Q_m(r)e^{-r/6}.
\]
The first region is uniformly bounded, and the second region is bounded by $C\nu^{1/6}e^{-\nu/12}$; the outer exponential estimate is smaller still. The finitely many low values of $m$ are absorbed into the constant. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CL}
C_L:=\sup_{m\ge1,\,x\in\R}\left|x e^{-x^2}L_m^{(1/2)}(3x^2/2)\right|<\infty.
\end{equation}

Fix $0<\epsilon<1/4$, let $\phi$ be the standard Gaussian density, and define
\[
p_m(x):=x e^{-x^2}L_m^{(1/2)}(3x^2/2),
\qquad h_m(x):=\frac{p_m(x)}{C_L},
\qquad f_m(x):=\phi(x)(1+\epsilon h_m(x)).
\]
By \eqref{eq:CL}, $f_m$ is a nonnegative probability density. It is normalized because $h_m$ is odd. The change of variables $r=3x^2/2$ and Laguerre orthogonality show that
\[
\int_\R x^{2k+1}h_m(x)\phi(x)\,dx=0,
\qquad 0\le k<m.
\]
In particular, the corresponding variable $U_m$ is centered. Its variance is one because $x^2h_m(x)\phi(x)$ is odd. The Laguerre generating identity gives the exact MGF
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laguerre-mgf}
M_{U_m}(s)=e^{s^2/2}(1+u_m(s)),
\qquad
u_m(s)=\frac{\epsilon}{C_L}\frac{s}{3\sqrt3}e^{-s^2/3}\frac{(-s^2/6)^m}{m!}.
\end{equation}
Therefore, with
\[
a_m:=\frac{\epsilon}{3\sqrt3 C_L6^m m!},
\]
we have $|u_m(s)|\le a_m|s|^{2m+1}$ on the real axis.

Let $J_m=N_m^+-N_m^-$, where $N_m^+$ and $N_m^-$ are independent Poisson variables with mean $\lambda_m/2$, and set
\[
\lambda_m:=96a_m^2\rho^{4m-2},
\qquad
W_m:=\frac{U_m+J_m}{\sqrt{1+\lambda_m}}.
\]
Then $W_m$ is centered and variance one. If $q=s/\sqrt{1+\lambda_m}$, its reflected defect is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laguerre-defect}
R_{W_m}(s)
 =\log(1-u_m(q)^2)
 +2\lambda_m\left(\cosh q-1-\frac{q^2}{2}\right).
\end{equation}
For all sufficiently large $m$, $|u_m(q)|\le1/2$ on $|s|\le\rho$. Using $-\log(1-v)\le2v$ for $0\le v\le1/4$ and $\cosh q-1-q^2/2\ge q^4/24$, the choice of $\lambda_m$ gives
\[
R_{W_m}(s)\ge\frac{\lambda_m q^4}{16}\ge0.
\]
The logarithmic term in \eqref{eq:laguerre-defect} is nonpositive, so the upper Taylor bound gives
\[
R_{W_m}(s)\le\frac{\lambda_m\cosh(\rho)q^4}{12}.
\]
If also $\lambda_m\le1$, evaluation at $s=\rho$ yields
\[
\frac{\lambda_m\rho^4}{64}\le\Delta_m:=\Delta_\rho(W_m)
\le\frac{\lambda_m\rho^4\cosh(\rho)}{12}.
\]
Thus $\Delta_m\asymp\lambda_m$ and Stirling's formula gives
\[
\log\frac1{\Delta_m}=2m\log m+O(m).
\]

The fixed exponential envelope is uniform: $f_m\le(1+\epsilon)\phi$, while
\[
\E e^{b|J_m|}\le2e^{\lambda_m(\cosh b-1)}
\]
and $\sup_m\lambda_m<\infty$. Finally, the half-axis discrepancy is explicit. Since
\[
\int_0^\infty h_m(x)\phi(x)\,dx
 =\frac{1}{3C_L\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{(1/2)_m}{m!},
\]
we have $|F_{U_m}(0)-1/2|\asymp m^{-1/2}$. Moreover,
\[
|F_{U_m+J_m}(0)-F_{U_m}(0)|\le\Pp(J_m\ne0)\le\lambda_m=o(m^{-1/2}).
\]
The normalization in $W_m$ leaves the threshold zero unchanged. Consequently
\[
d_{\mathrm K}(W_m,\Normal(0,1))\ge c m^{-1/2}.
\]
Together with $\log(1/\Delta_m)=2m\log m+O(m)$, this proves Theorem~\ref{thm:sharpness}. It also rules out every uniform power-law modulus $C\Delta^\alpha$, $\alpha>0$, and rules out the previously proposed $C/\sqrt{\log(1/\Delta)}$ bound.

\subsection{Failure of total-variation stability}

Let $N_\lambda\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)$ and $X_\lambda=(N_\lambda-\lambda)/\sqrt\lambda$. The reflected defect satisfies, on every fixed compact $s$-interval,
\[
R_{X_\lambda}(s)=\frac{s^4}{12\lambda}+O_\rho(\lambda^{-2}),
\]
and the family has a fixed exponential-integrability bound for each fixed $\tau$. Nevertheless $X_\lambda$ is lattice-valued, whereas $\Normal(0,1)$ is absolutely continuous, so
\[
d_{\rm TV}(X_\lambda,\Normal(0,1))=1.
\]
This is a qualitative boundary phenomenon and is independent of the Kolmogorov lower bound above.
