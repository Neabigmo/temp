\title{Symmetrized-MGF Gaussian Rigidity: Chi-Square Quadratic Forms and Sharp Reflection Stability}

\author{Author Name(s)}

\date{}

\begin{abstract}
We study Gaussian rigidity through the reflection-symmetrized moment generating function (MGF). For a centered, variance-one random variable $X$ with a fixed exponential moment, write $M_X$ for its MGF and set
\[
R_X(s)=\log M_X(s)+\log M_X(-s)-s^2,
\qquad \Delta_\rho(X)=\sup_{|s|\le\rho}|R_X(s)|.
\]
We prove an exact rigidity theorem for positive-semidefinite quadratic forms of independent coordinates under the one-sided condition $R_{X_i}(s)\ge0$, and we establish a quantitative reflection-stability theorem that only assumes $|R_X(s)|\le\Delta$ on a fixed interval. Specifically, sufficiently small $\Delta_\rho(X)$ implies
\[
d_{\mathrm K}(X,N(0,1))
\le C\sqrt{\frac{\log\log(1/\Delta_\rho(X))}{\log(1/\Delta_\rho(X))}}.
\]
The exact quadratic-form result and a sample-variance corollary explain how this one-dimensional reflection problem arises from spherical averaging. The upper-bound mechanism converts local reflected-MGF control into high-order moment information and then recovers the factor through a growing zero-free region. The Laguerre construction supplies the matching lower-order mechanism, while a lattice family rules out total-variation stability.
\end{abstract}
